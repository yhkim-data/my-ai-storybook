The rights to the book you make belong to you. This tool and DI Lab claim no rights to anything you create.

- The name of this tool and the name DI Lab do not appear inside your book.
- You may show, share, and print the book you made.
- This tool never asks for permission or money for your book.

## The AI notice in your book

- If even one picture was made with AI, the "Made by" page of your book automatically says "Some pictures were made with AI (name)". The name of the AI that drew the pictures goes in the parentheses.
- If all pictures are your own uploads, this line is not added.
- Pictures made by Google's picture AI carry an invisible SynthID watermark. The official document says "All generated images include a SynthID watermark." (Checked 2026-11-14. Source: https://aistudio.google.com/docs/image-generation)

## The AI company's terms

The AI this tool connects to is Google's Gemini. The terms (Gemini API Additional Terms of Service, effective March 23, 2026) say this. (Checked 2026-11-14. Source: https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)

> "Some of our Services allow you to generate original content. Google won't claim ownership over that content."

- Google does not claim ownership of the words and pictures the AI makes.
- But whether AI-made pictures are protected by copyright differs from country to country. This tool cannot decide how the law will treat them.
- If you want to sell the book or enter it in a contest, check the rules of your country and of that contest.
- For the age requirement and privacy points about using AI, see [How to get a key](key-guide.en.md) and the [teacher guide](teacher-guide.en.md).

## When you use someone else's pictures or characters

- To add a picture or character made by someone else, you need that person's permission.
- A picture you found on the internet is not free to use just because you found it.
- Do not add characters from comics, films, or games, or pictures that show a brand mark.
- If you add a picture made in another AI app, follow that app's terms of use.
- If a photo shows another person's face, get that person's permission. For photos of children, you need a parent's or guardian's permission.
